For Agency Owners and Principals

The Agency AEP Readiness Checklist

A seven-part operating review for agency owners. Run it before October 15.

Certification gets your agents to the starting line. This checklist gets your whole agency there.

Score My Agency

AEP does not reward the busiest agency. It rewards the most prepared one.

The Annual Enrollment Period runs October 15 through December 7. Those 54 days can decide your agency's year. Most owners make sure their agents finish certification. Far fewer pressure-test the rest of the business: the book of business, the lead plan, the systems, the compliance file, and the team.

That gap is where AEP breaks down. Not in October. In July and August, when no one asked the hard questions.

This checklist gives you those questions. It covers seven parts of your operation. Each section tells you what to verify, who should own it, and how to prove it is done. You should not complete these tasks yourself. Your job is to assign each one, set a deadline, and check the evidence.

How to use this guide

  1. Complete the Readiness Snapshot below.
  2. Assign each of the seven sections to an accountable owner.
  3. Review progress weekly. Update the scorecard as items close.
  4. Hold the 30-day go or no-go review before October 15.
GreenComplete, tested, and documented.

If AEP started next Monday, where would your agency break first? Write your answer down. Then read that section first.

1
Contracting and Certification

Every producer, ready to sell, before the first lead is routed

The goal: Prove that every producer can legally and operationally sell the products you plan to assign them, in every market you plan to assign, before leads and appointments go out.

Certification season starts in early summer. The agencies that finish early get a bonus most owners miss: time to test carrier systems and fix problems while it is still quiet.

0 of 10 complete
Worksheet: Producer Readiness MatrixTrack each producer in one grid: name, NPN, state, carrier, product, certification complete, ready-to-sell verified, portal access tested, issue owner, and target fix date.
Owner checkpoint

Can you prove that every lead will route only to a producer who is ready to sell that product in that market?

2
Book of Business Review

Your book is your best AEP asset. Treat it like one.

The goal: Turn your book of business into a prioritized service and retention plan, not a last-minute call list.

Plans change every year. Costs, provider networks, pharmacy networks, and benefits can all shift. CMS urges beneficiaries to review their Annual Notice of Change each fall. The agencies that reach those clients first, with a real needs-based review, keep them.

0 of 10 complete
Worksheet: Book of Business Segmentation PlanFor each segment: estimated volume, reason for review, outreach window, assigned team, script or template, and completion metric.
Owner checkpoint

Does every client segment have a clear reason for outreach, one assigned owner, and enough capacity to complete a meaningful review?

3
Marketing and Lead Generation

Buy demand your team can actually answer

The goal: Build a compliant demand plan that matches your appointment and follow-up capacity.

More leads are not always more sales. Leads your team cannot call back quickly are wasted spend. Executive discipline here means working backward from capacity, not forward from budget.

0 of 13 complete
Worksheet: AEP Demand and Capacity PlanFor each channel: budget, lead target, expected appointments, responsible team, launch date, approval status, tracking method, and a stop-or-scale rule.
Owner checkpoint

If lead volume doubles for three days, does your agency respond faster, or does the extra spend just build a bigger backlog?

4
Technology and CRM

Systems that work without heroics

The goal: Verify that your systems can capture, route, document, enroll, and report without relying on memory or manual rescue.

During AEP, a broken form or a silent integration does not just cost a lead. It can cost the consent record and the audit trail behind it. Test everything now, end to end, while a failure is still cheap.

0 of 11 complete
Worksheet: AEP Systems Test ScriptFor each scenario: expected result, actual result, pass or fail, issue owner, fix date, and retest date.
Owner checkpoint

Can you trace one lead from source through enrollment and service without rebuilding the story from spreadsheets and inboxes?

5
Compliance Review and Reminders

Compliance is an operating system, not a memo

The goal: Turn compliance into a documented process that protects beneficiaries and gives your agents clear rules for outreach, appointments, events, and enrollment.

Strong agencies do not treat compliance as a brake on growth. They treat it as proof of quality. Every item below should live in a file you could hand to a carrier or regulator on request.

0 of 15 complete
Worksheet: Compliance File AuditFor each requirement: policy location, responsible owner, sample evidence reviewed, gap found, corrective action, and completion date.
Owner checkpoint

Could your agency produce the documentation behind a lead, an appointment, a recommendation, and an enrollment if a carrier or regulator asked for it?

6
Sales and Retention Strategy

Sell for the client's fit, and keep the client you enrolled

The goal: Run a needs-based sales process that supports responsible growth and long-term retention.

Volume without fit creates rapid disenrollment, complaints, and chargebacks. The strongest agencies coach on fit, accuracy, and client experience, not just application counts. Retention is where AEP work turns into durable revenue.

0 of 12 complete
Worksheet: Sales Quality ScorecardFor each producer: appointments, show rate, applications, enrollment status, error rate, complaints, pending cases, follow-up completion, and early retention indicators.
Owner checkpoint

Are your agents coached only on production volume, or also on fit, documentation, accuracy, and client experience?

7
Team and Process Readiness

When volume spikes, clarity wins

The goal: Make responsibilities, handoffs, escalation paths, and backup coverage clear before volume increases.

Every AEP failure story ends the same way: nobody knew who owned the decision. Fix that in September, on paper, and October gets much calmer.

0 of 12 complete
Worksheet: AEP Responsibility MapFor each process: accountable owner, supporting roles, backup owner, service-level expectation, escalation trigger, and documentation location.
Owner checkpoint

When something breaks during AEP, will your team know who owns the decision and what happens next?

The 30-Day Go or No-Go Review

One meeting, held about 30 days before October 15. Put it on the calendar today. Bring your leadership team and the completed scorecard.

Agenda

  1. Review every red and yellow item.
  2. Confirm every gap has one accountable owner and a deadline.
  3. Retest any failed systems and workflows.
  4. Validate producer readiness against your planned lead routing.
  5. Compare planned campaign volume with actual appointment capacity.
  6. Review compliance updates and carrier-specific changes.
  7. Confirm backup plans for technology, staffing, and vendor issues.
  8. Decide what you will delay, reduce, or pause if gaps remain.

Final sign-off questions

  • Are all producers ready to sell for their assigned markets and products?
  • Is the book of business segmented and assigned?
  • Is marketing approved, scheduled, measurable, and matched to capacity?
  • Have lead sources and consent workflows been reviewed?
  • Have critical systems passed an end-to-end test?
  • Can you document compliance for outreach, appointments, and enrollment?
  • Are sales, service, and retention processes staffed and measurable?
  • Does every critical workflow have an owner and a backup?

If you can answer yes to all eight, your agency is ready. If not, you know exactly where to focus.
